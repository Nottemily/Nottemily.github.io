If there's one material we can't seem to live without, it's plastic.

 

There is a reason for that: It's cheap, durable, and lightweight, making it perfect for everything from iPhones to eye wear.

The Facts
What can I do?

 

Its convenient, sure.

 

But what makes plastic so useful for humans is exactly what makes it a nasty environmental contaminate—it spreads easily and takes forever to degrade, finding its way to our lands and oceans where it wreaks havoc on wildlife.

It spreads easily and takes forever to degrade, finding its way to our lands and oceans where it wreaks havoc on wildlife. To date, at least 700 species of marine animals, including shorebirds, have been entangled by plastic or mistaken it for food. The result is often suffocation or starvation.

Since the 1950s, we've generated 8.3 billion metric tons of the stuff, of which a scant nine percent has been recycled. And by 2050, scientists predict the oceans will stock more plastic than fish.

Here are the facts..

The world generates over 400 million tonnes of plastic waste a year.

The world's biggest contributor of plastic pollution is none other than the US. The country, which is already one of the world's top contributor to greenhouse gas emissions, generates about 42 million metric tons of plastic waste each year

 

More than 8 million tonnes of plastic enter the oceans every year.

 

When plastic waste is dumped into landfills or irresponsibly discarded, significant amounts are leaked into natural environments and at least eight million tonnes of plastic ends up in ocean waters every single year. It is estimated that roughly 40% of the ocean's surface is covered in plastic debris and if our plastic consumption and behavior continues, scientists warn that there will be more plastic than fish in the ocean as soon as 2030.

Humans ingests 5 grams of plastic every week.

According to a 2019 Reuters infographic, humans ingest about five grams of plastic every week - a spoonful that weighs about as much as a bottle cap and equivalent to about half a rice bowl. Combined throughout a year, the amount of plastic we ingest amounts to a full dinner plate.

100,000 animals die from plastic entanglement each year.

Between 500,000 and one million tons of ghost fishing equipment [fishing gear that are lost, abandoned or discarded left by fishermen] enters the ocean each year. These equipment are either dumped from fishing boats or washed away from ships and shorelines. As a result, at least 100,000 marine animals die from getting entangled in fishing litter, from either asphyxiation or trapped from traveling for food and are starved to death.

But as problematic and worrisome as all of this is, completely cutting plastic from our lives is nearly impossible at this point. Reducing your plastic use, however, is surprisingly easy and pain-free. There are many small ways you can have a big impact.

5 ways you can help

 

Buy in bulk and look for eco-friendly packaging.

Single-serving yogurts, tiny packages of nuts; consider the product-to-packaging ratio of items you tend to buy often and select the bigger container instead of buying several smaller ones over time. Look for opportunities to buy staples like rice and pasta from bulk containers and store them in jars at home. When you have the option, choose products in nonplastic packaging (e.g. go for the glass jar of honey, not the squeezable honey bear).

1

 

Choose clothing made from natural fibers.

 

An estimated 9 percent of the microplastics in the ocean are coming from synthetic fibers—the kind found in materials like nylon, acrylic, polyester, and fleece. They're shed from the clothes we wear, and make their way from our laundry machines to wastewater plants, before spreading throughout the environment.

When possible, choose clothing and textiles made of natural materials, like cotton and wool, instead. Shop at a local farmers market - farmers markets are a great way to buy fresh, local produce without plastic, as long as you remember to bring your own bags.

2

No more take-out, cook more.

Have you ever tried to cut a piece of broccoli with a plastic fork? Yeah, not fun. And yet Americans use 100 million plastic utensils everyday, much of which comes wrapped in even more plastic. Better yet: Try cooking for yourself. Not only is it healthier but making your own meals doesn't involve takeout containers or plastic bags. For those times when you do order in or eat out, tell the establishment you don't need any plastic cutlery or plastic carry-out bags.

3

 

Remember Your Reusable Bag

 

Look, you probably already have plenty of reusable bags, but the tricky part is remembering to take them anywhere. Store one in everything you take with you—your purse, backpack, gym bag—and if you drive to the store, in your car.

4

 

Get Better at Recycling

The shine of new toys, trinkets, and electronic gadgets wears off fast. Search the shelves of thrift stores, neighborhood garage sales, or online postings for items (plastic and otherwise) that are just as good when previously used. For things you rarely need, consider borrowing before you decide to buy new. Not only will you consume less plastic when you start making these efforts to reduce and reuse more in your life, you'll save yourself a few bucks, too.

5

 

Whether you're participating in Plastic Free July or simply making a change in your life, these are just some of the small, but effective ways to reduce your plastic waste impact on the planet.
If everyone were more mindful of the plastic waste crisis plaguing the planet, we could start to undo some of the damage we've done in the name of convenience.
